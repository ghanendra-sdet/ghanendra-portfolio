Overview

The platform lets travelers search, compare, and book flights, hotels, and packages across many suppliers through one unified interface. Because the platform doesn't own the inventory it sells, its core testing challenge is guaranteeing that a fare-locked price is honored exactly, and that two travelers can never both "win" the same last seat or room.

In plain terms, this is a storefront reselling inventory it doesn't own — prices and availability are controlled by external suppliers and can change at any moment, so the platform has to guarantee what a traveler is quoted is exactly what they're charged. How It Works & My Role

Core Booking Flow

  1. Traveler searches flights, hotels, or packages with filters
  2. Selecting an option triggers a Fare Lock, holding the price and inventory unit
  3. Payment completed within the hold window charges exactly the locked price
  4. Booking is confirmed and a PNR is issued
  5. Cancellations are processed per fare-type-specific Refund Policy Tiers

Why Overbooking Prevention Is a Concurrency Problem

Two travelers can attempt to book the last available seat within milliseconds of each other — only one may ever succeed, which makes this fundamentally a concurrency-correctness test, not a sequential functional one.

Challenges & Problem-Solving

Two representative problems from this module — how they were found and resolved, not just what the final test covered.

Two travelers both confirming the last available room
Challenge

Two travelers could both receive a confirmed booking for the same last-available room when they fare-locked within milliseconds of each other.

Approach

Designed a deliberate concurrency test racing two simultaneous booking attempts against one inventory unit, rather than testing bookings sequentially.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-TRV-2011 (Critical); the fix required an atomic, database-level check at confirmation time, not just at initial selection.

Payment succeeding at a price that had already expired
Challenge

Payment succeeded at a stale, expired fare-locked price because the hold-expiry check only ran client-side, via the visible countdown timer.

Approach

Tested payment submission specifically after letting the hold window elapse without refreshing, rather than trusting the countdown display as the source of truth.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-TRV-2027; the fix moved expiry enforcement server-side, independent of the client's countdown timer.
