Healthcare — Claim Insurance Platform

A SaaS health insurance claims platform spanning four entity types — Providers, Payers, Employers & Members — where a single claim's consistency across all four views is the platform's core trust guarantee.

Overview

The platform manages the entire health insurance lifecycle — enrollment through claims processing to billing — across four distinct entity types, each with its own portal and view of the same underlying claim. The highest-value defects are consistency gaps between these views: a claim shown as Final to the Member but still Need Review on the Payer's side is a trust-breaking bug, not a cosmetic one.

In plain terms, this platform is a four-sided marketplace built around one core financial transaction — a claim. A Provider delivers care and submits it, a Payer decides whether and how much to pay, an Employer's plan defines what's covered, and a Member is the person the whole thing is ultimately for. How It Works & My Role

Core Claim Lifecycle

  1. Member enrolls under an Employer plan, or individually
  2. Member receives care from a Provider
  3. Provider submits a claim on the Member's behalf
  4. Payer reviews the claim against the Employer's plan coverage rules
  5. Claim resolves to Final, Need Review, or Rejected

Why Cross-Entity Consistency Is the Central Risk

The same claim is visible, in different forms, to all four entities — a status shown correctly to one but stale to another silently breaks trust across the whole platform, which is why data-level validation (not just UI assertions) is a first-class testing practice here.

Challenges & Problem-Solving

Two representative problems from this module — how they were found and resolved, not just what the final test covered.

Member portal showing a status the Payer had already changed
Challenge

The Member portal showed a claim as Final while the Payer portal still showed Need Review, because the Member view was reading from a stale cached copy of the claim status.

Approach

Tested claim-status consistency across all four portals immediately after a status change, rather than after a delay that would let caches quietly catch up.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-HIP-6014 (Critical); the fix required the Member portal to read live status or use a tightly bounded cache TTL, not an independently-cached copy.

A rejection reason recorded but not displayed everywhere
Challenge

A rejection reason was correctly recorded by the Payer but showed blank in the Provider portal, even though the Member portal displayed it correctly.

Approach

Traced the gap to a newer schema field that had only been wired into some of the four portals' detail views, not all of them.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-HIP-6032; reinforced testing every claim-status field across all four portals whenever the schema changes, not just the primary one.
