Overview

BBPS lets a merchant fetch and pay any registered biller's bill (electricity, water, gas, DTH, telecom) through one standardized interface. The platform doesn't own the billers' data, so its core risk is biller-integration reliability — and it offers merchants a choice between paying via an external Payment Gateway or the platform's own Payout/Connected Banking rail at a lower fee.

In plain terms, BBPS is a universal bill-pay counter bolted onto the platform — instead of a merchant's customer visiting each biller's own portal, they fetch and pay through one consistent flow, while the platform silently resolves which payment rail is cheapest for that merchant. The module is built from a smaller, focused set of services layered on top of the shared platform:

How It Works & My Role

Core Bill Payment Flow

  1. User selects a bill category (electricity, water, gas, DTH, etc.)
  2. The platform fetches the live outstanding amount from the biller
  3. Payment rail is resolved: internal Payout/Connected Banking (lower fee) if eligible, else external Gateway
  4. User pays the fetched amount via the resolved rail
  5. Transaction status is tracked to a definitive outcome
  6. Settlement and Reports reflect the completed payment

Why Stale-Amount Protection Matters

The platform doesn't own the billers' data — if time passes between fetch and payment, it must re-validate or clearly flag the amount as stale, never silently charge an outdated figure. The same discipline applies to picking the correct payment rail and fee.

Challenges & Problem-Solving

Two representative problems from this module — how they were found and resolved, not just what the final test covered.

Merchant overcharged the external Gateway fee despite an active internal rail
Challenge

A merchant whose Payout/Connected Banking service had lapsed and been reinstated was still being routed to the more expensive external Gateway, because the rail-selection check used cached rather than live eligibility.

Approach

Traced the defect to a historical-eligibility flag rather than a live status check, and built a regression test specifically for the reinstatement scenario.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-BBPS-1122; the fix required checking live service status at the moment of rail selection, not a cached flag.

Defining "stale" precisely enough to test it
Challenge

Bill amounts fetched from a biller could go stale if a customer paused before completing payment, but "stale" needed a precise, testable definition rather than a vague notion of "too long."

Approach

Defined an explicit freshness window and built tests around fetching, waiting past the window, and then attempting payment.

Outcome

Confirmed the platform correctly blocked or re-validated stale amounts rather than silently charging an outdated figure.
