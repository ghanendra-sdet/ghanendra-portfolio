Overview

Learnfinity is a multi-tenant LMS for universities and trainers — course management, mock tests, certifications, and Zoom/Teams integration. Because a certificate is a credential, the platform's core trust guarantee is that "Completed" reflects genuine engagement: a learner seeking a video to its end must never satisfy the same completion checkpoints as actually watching it.

In plain terms, Learnfinity is a school built into software — a course isn't "done" just because the progress bar hit 100%, it's done when the platform can prove genuine content engagement AND a passing assessment score, independently. Underneath the course player, the platform is composed of these core modules:

The hardest part of testing this: proving genuine engagement — not just a UI end-state — requires reasoning about playback events over time (checkpoints hit via forward play, not a scrubber jump), which is a fundamentally different testing problem than asserting a final state.

How It Works & My Role

Core Completion Flow

  1. Learner enrolls in a course and consumes content (video / reading / SCORM)
  2. Content completion requires genuine playback through required checkpoints
  3. Learner takes an assessment (auto-graded quiz or manually-graded assignment)
  4. A certificate is issued only when both content-consumption AND a passing score are independently met

Why Two Independent Gates Matter

Content-consumption completion and a passing assessment score are independent gates — a certificate must never be issued on partial satisfaction of either one alone, since it represents a claim the learner actually engaged with and passed the material.

Challenges & Problem-Solving

Two representative problems from this module — how they were found and resolved, not just what the final test covered.

A video marked complete without being watched
Challenge

Seeking a video lesson directly to its final timestamp marked it Complete, without the learner actually watching any of the required content.

Approach

Tested completion logic by deliberately seeking rather than playing through, distinguishing "reached the end" from "watched it."

Outcome

Filed as BUG-LMS-4015 (Critical); the fix required checkpoints to be satisfied via forward-playback events, not just a final-timestamp check.

A skipped question inflating the quiz score
Challenge

A 9-of-10-correct quiz scored 100% instead of 90% when one question was left unanswered, because the grading engine excluded unanswered questions from the denominator.

Approach

Tested a deliberately-skipped-question scenario rather than only fully-answered quizzes.

Outcome

Filed as BUG-LMS-4032; the fix calculated score against total questions, treating an unanswered question as incorrect.
