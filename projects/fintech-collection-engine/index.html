Overview

Collection Engine covers the full merchant collection lifecycle — onboarding through settlement — across five distinct collection rails. A core testing challenge is that every rail ultimately touches the same ledger, so correctness has to be validated at the data layer, not just the UI: a missing ledger debit or a GST rounding error is real money out of balance, not a cosmetic bug.

In plain terms, Collection is the side of the platform that brings money in — it lets a merchant accept payments from their own customers via UPI, QR, Virtual Accounts, Payment Links, or Manual Deposit, tracks each payment from initiation to a definitive status, and reconciles every rupee against the merchant's ledger. Underneath the UI, the module is composed of roughly 40 cooperating microservices, each owning one narrow slice of that journey:

How It Works & My Role

Core Collection Flow

  1. Merchant initiates a collection via UPI, QR, Virtual Account, Payment Link, or Manual Deposit
  2. The Collection Transaction Service records the attempt and tracks its status
  3. Payment Processing confirms funds received from the payer
  4. The Commercial & GST Engine calculates MDR fees and tax
  5. The Settlement Service credits the merchant's ledger balance
  6. Dashboards and Reports reflect the updated, reconciled balance

Challenges & Problem-Solving

Two representative problems from this module — how they were found and resolved, not just what the final test covered.

Missing ledger debit entries at the Settlement → Ledger boundary
Challenge

The Settlement Calculation Service occasionally produced a missing ledger debit entry when a collection succeeded but fee finalization hadn't completed yet — a timing gap invisible to isolated, single-service tests.

Approach

Designed integration tests that deliberately raced settlement calculation against fee finalization, rather than testing each service independently.

Outcome

Caught the sequencing gap before release; missing-ledger-debit became a named, permanently tracked regression category.

GST rounding mismatches at specific transaction amounts
Challenge

GST rounding differences appeared only at specific amounts (e.g. values ending in .005), caused by the Commercial Engine and GST Engine using different rounding modes.

Approach

Built targeted boundary-value test data around rounding thresholds instead of relying on random or representative sample amounts.

Outcome

Identified the rounding-mode mismatch as a systemic issue rather than a one-off, prompting a shared rounding-utility fix.
